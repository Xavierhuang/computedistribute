# Market Size, Growth, and Buyer Demand for Rentable GPU/CPU Compute (as of 2026-11-08)

Context: background for a two-sided marketplace where people buy and sell spare or idle compute. All figures are labeled with their date. "Neocloud" means GPU-first cloud providers such as CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda, and Crusoe.

**Source-quality note.** Almost all "GPU-as-a-Service market size" numbers come from paid-report vendors (Grand View, Meticulous, Roots, Straits, Persistence, Fortune Business Insights, Mordor), mostly seen through press-release syndication or aggregator pages. Treat them as low-to-medium quality. The best demand evidence is in company filings and earnings (CoreWeave, Nebius), the SemiAnalysis rental-price index, and Messari for DePIN.

---

## 1. Market size and growth forecasts: GPU-as-a-Service (GPUaaS) / GPU cloud and decentralized compute (DePIN)

### Takeaway
Analyst firms put GPUaaS at roughly $5–11B in 2026. Their forecasts range from about $14B to over $160B by the early-to-mid 2030s, because each firm defines the market differently. These figures are far smaller than what the neoclouds alone are booking: CoreWeave's 2026 revenue guidance is $12.4–13.2B. That gap means the analyst numbers undercount the market, and actual revenue and backlog are a better measure of size. DePIN compute is tiny by revenue (onchain revenue is in the tens of millions of dollars per year across all of DePIN), even though its token market cap is about $10B.

### Inferences
- The GPUaaS forecasts from analyst vendors are not useful for sizing. One public neocloud's 2026 revenue (about $13B) is larger than most of their 2026 market-size figures. A defensible top-down frame is "GPU cloud rental is a $25B+ revenue market in 2026, growing more than 100% YoY at the leaders." That figure is my sum of the CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda, Crusoe, and Together numbers below, before counting hyperscaler GPU revenue.
- Decentralized/DePIN compute is real but small. Verified onchain revenue is in the tens of millions of dollars per year across all DePIN, which is under 1% of neocloud revenue. The opening for a new marketplace is the long tail of buyers and consumer/prosumer GPUs, not taking share from frontier training clusters.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, methodology-transparent estimate of the size of the "marketplace / spot / peer-to-peer GPU" segment specifically (Vast.ai, RunPod Community, Salad, TensorDock). Vast.ai and RunPod revenue are not publicly disclosed in anything I found.
- I could not access the primary Messari "State of DePIN 2025" report (messari.io) to break out compute-only revenue.
- Gartner and IDC GPU-cloud forecasts were not surfaced, and I did not find a16z or Galaxy Research public estimates in the time available.

## 2. Size of the leading neoclouds (where demand is concentrated)

### Takeaway
Demand is heavily concentrated in a few neoclouds that serve hyperscalers and frontier labs on multi-year take-or-pay contracts. CoreWeave has about $104B of backlog, and about 67% of its 2025 revenue came from Microsoft. Nebius is the fastest-growing public neocloud. Lambda, Crusoe, and Together are each heading toward roughly $1–2B in revenue in 2026, but those figures come from investor talk, not audited results.

### Cited Findings
- **CoreWeave Q2 2026** (reported Aug 11 2026): revenue about **$2.575B, +112% YoY** (from $1.212B). Revenue backlog is **$104.2B, +246% YoY** ($103.7B of RPO plus $0.5B other), and that excludes **$25B+ of new commitments signed in early Q3**. Q3 2026 revenue guidance is $3.45–3.6B. FY26 revenue guidance is $12.4–13.2B. 2026 capex is guided at $35–39B. — [CoreWeave IR](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx); [pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coreweave-revenue-backlog-reaches-104-billion-before-another-25-billion-of-new-commitments/)
- **CoreWeave concentration:** about **67% of 2025 revenue came from Microsoft** (search summary of filings). OpenAI is also a major contracted customer. — [CoreWeave Q2 2026 coverage](https://longyield.substack.com/p/coreweave-books-104-billion-it-also)
- **CoreWeave description:** the company called its capacity "effectively sold out" and raised list prices by about 25% in July 2026. This comes from secondary aggregation. — [cloudmagazin, Oct 2026](https://www.cloudmagazin.com/en/2026/10/04/renting-gpus-without-hyperscalers-assessing-the-value-of-neo-clouds/)
- **For comparison, older data:** CoreWeave Q3 2025 revenue was $1.36B with a $55.6B backlog. — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/coreweave-q3-earnings-show-revenue-backlog-doubled-to-556bn/)
- **Nebius Q2 2026:** revenue **$582.3M, +454% YoY**. ARR was **$3.0B in June 2026**. FY2026 guidance is **$3.0–3.4B**, with an end-2026 ARR target of **$7–9B**. — [Nebius coverage via search; SII analysis](https://www.sii.org.pl/19312/analizy/analizy-rynki-zagraniczne/nebius-group-po-wynikach-2q-2026-skala-biznesu-rosnie-szybciej-niz-bardzo-wysokie-oczekiwania-analiza.html). Older data: Q3 2025 revenue was $146M and ARR was $551M in Sept 2025. — [rallies.ai](https://rallies.ai/news/nebius-group-secures-over-20-billion-backlog-scales-ai-capacity-to-220-mw)
- **Lambda** (private): 2026 revenue is reportedly "on track to top **$1.5B**" (Bloomberg-sourced). It is reportedly in talks for a $3B pre-IPO round at a $12B+ valuation. 2025 revenue was about $520M, per an aggregator. — [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nvidia-backed-lambda-in-talks-for-3b-pre-ipo-round-at-12b-valuation-as-2026); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/lambda-labs/financials). These are expectations, not reported results.
- **Crusoe** (private): it reportedly told investors it expects about **$2.2B** of 2026 revenue. Sacra earlier estimated $276M (2024–25 era). The sources disagree. — [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-top-revenue); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/crusoe-at-276m-revenue/)
- **Together AI** (private): reportedly has **$1.15B+ in annual bookings**, with outside estimates putting annualized revenue around $1B (2026). ARR was about $300M in Sept 2025 (older). — [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-infrastructure-top-revenue); [nexairi](https://nexairi-mentis.pages.dev/article/Technology/together-ai-1b-funding-7.5b-valuation). Bookings are not revenue.
- **Q3 2026 earnings:** CoreWeave and Nebius historically report Q3 in mid-November (CoreWeave on Nov 10, 2025; Nebius on Nov 11, 2025). As of 2026-11-08 I found no Q3 2026 results.

### Inferences
- The top two public neoclouds alone are on track for about $16B of 2026 revenue. Adding Lambda, Crusoe, and Together gives roughly $20B+, growing about 100–450% YoY. Demand is dominated by large, long-term contracts from hyperscalers and labs, which take up the premium supply (H100/H200/B200/GB200 clusters with InfiniBand).
- That concentration leaves an underserved long tail: small buyers who cannot sign multi-year, prepaid, 64-plus-GPU contracts. A spare-compute marketplace would serve that group.

### Gaps
- Q3 2026 results for CoreWeave and Nebius are not yet available.
- Revenue for Lambda, Crusoe, Together, RunPod, and Vast.ai is unaudited or undisclosed. I found no reliable RunPod or Vast.ai revenue figures.
- I did not verify a 2026 customer-concentration percentage for CoreWeave.

## 3. Training vs inference share of AI compute demand

### Takeaway
The most-cited estimate is Deloitte's: inference was about half of AI compute in 2025 and about two-thirds in 2026, up from about one-third in 2023. Many secondary sources repeat this one estimate, so it is less independently confirmed than it looks. SemiAnalysis (April 2026) attributes the 2026 rental squeeze largely to agentic and inference demand.

### Cited Findings
- Deloitte TMT Predictions 2026 (published Nov–Dec 2025): inference will be **about two-thirds of AI compute in 2026**, up from **one-third in 2023** and **half in 2025**. Deloitte expects most of it to stay in data centres and enterprise servers rather than move to the edge. It also says post-training and test-time scaling are "compute hogs" that keep demand high. — [Deloitte press release](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-2026-tmt-predictions.html); [Deloitte Malta](https://www.deloitte.com/mt/en/about/press-room/2026-tmt-predictions.html); [Business Today, Mar 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/ais-next-phase-to-drive-higher-computing-demand-not-less-warns-deloitte-521227-2026-03-18)
- SemiAnalysis (Apr 2, 2026) names multi-agent workflows, coding agents (Claude Code), MoE inference, open-weight models, and native video and image generation as major drivers of the 2026 shortage. It also says H100s remain cost-effective for training, which keeps older cards in demand. — [SemiAnalysis, "The Great GPU Shortage"](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-great-gpu-shortage-rental-capacity)
- Vendor and blog claims that inference takes "80% of lifecycle spending" have no clear source. — [metavert](https://metavert.io/compare/ai-inference-vs-ai-model-training). Low quality.

### Inferences
- Inference, batch inference, and fine-tuning are the parts of the market that tolerate heterogeneous, geographically spread, single-node GPUs, which is the kind of supply a spare-compute marketplace has. The shift toward inference therefore helps marketplace compute, particularly for open-weight model serving, batch jobs, and image and video generation on 24–32GB consumer cards.
- Large pre-training still needs co-located clusters with InfiniBand or NVLink interconnect, which a peer-to-peer marketplace cannot easily supply.

### Gaps
- I found no measured (as opposed to forecast) split of training vs inference from SemiAnalysis, Epoch, or IDC in this pass.
- I found no split of inference demand by GPU class (consumer vs datacenter).

## 4. Buyer segments for cheap / spot / consumer-GPU compute and what they value

### Takeaway
Cheap marketplace compute is bought by price-sensitive buyers running jobs that tolerate interruption: indie developers and hobbyists running open-weight LLMs, researchers and students, fine-tuners, batch and fault-tolerant inference, image and video generation, and rendering. They value price first, then availability and fast provisioning. Reliability, trust/privacy, and multi-GPU interconnect are the main reasons buyers leave marketplaces for managed neoclouds.

### Cited Findings
**Segments and fit:**
- ComputePrices (Sept 2026): marketplaces fit **fault-tolerant inference and batch jobs** best, citing Salad's container model, along with **rendering** and **interactive development** (SSH pods on RunPod Community, Vast, Lium, QuickPod). Large training clusters are a **poor fit**: "A single cheap marketplace node cannot substitute for a cluster", and "Price floor is not scalable price." — [ComputePrices](https://computeprices.com/blog/gpu-marketplaces-compared-vast-runpod-salad-shadeform-and-more)
- ComputePrices also found that marketplace-style providers hold **7 of the 10 cheapest listings for RTX 5090 and RTX 4090**, but only **3/10 for H100 SXM** and **2/10 for B200**. Listing counts by GPU: RTX 4090 had 18 providers, RTX 5090 14, H100 SXM 48, and B200 28. Vast.ai lists 52 GPU models and Salad 37. — [ComputePrices](https://computeprices.com/blog/gpu-marketplaces-compared-vast-runpod-salad-shadeform-and-more)
- **Academics:** a 2024 Nature analysis (cited second-hand) found that most academics with compute access have **1–8 GPUs**, while frontier labs have thousands. — [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/sasha/compute-competition)
- A 2025 arXiv survey of 229 foundation-model researchers (312 papers) found that access to high-throughput infrastructure matters more than raw GPU count. — [arXiv 2510.13621](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13621v1)
- The CVPR 2026 compute report found that authors of accepted papers had better GPUs and more development time. — [CVPR 2026](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/News/ComputeReport)
- **Enterprises and startups:** in a survey of 1,367 AI professionals, **85%** said their organisation had delayed projects for lack of GPU access. — [Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/survey-gpu-scarcity-delays-ai-initiatives)
- Top challenges in scaling AI infrastructure were infra cost (**54%**), skilled staff (**43%**), and inference cost (**40%**). — [DataCentreNews UK](https://datacentrenews.uk/story/ai-firms-struggle-with-gpu-access-amid-rising-costs)
- GPU compute reportedly takes **40–60% of AI startups' technical budgets** in their first two years. This comes from GMI Cloud, a vendor, so it is unverified. — [GMI Cloud](https://www.gmicloud.ai/blog/how-much-do-gpu-cloud-platforms-cost-for-ai-startups-in-2025)
- **AI natives and smaller labs** now mostly sign 1–3 year terms, and increasingly 4+ year deals with more than 20% prepayment. This raises the barrier for small buyers on neoclouds. — [SemiAnalysis Apr 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-great-gpu-shortage-rental-capacity)

**What buyers value:**
- **Price:**
  - Late-Oct 2026 on-demand H100 prices: **Vast.ai $2.14/hr**; RunPod $3.99 (SXM) or $2.89 (PCIe); Lambda $3.99; Nebius $4.50; CoreWeave $6.16; AWS $6.88; Oracle $10.00; GCP $11.06; Azure $12.29.
  - Consumer cards: **RTX 4090 from about $0.30/hr** on Vast versus $0.89 on RunPod, and **RTX 5090 from about $0.50/hr** on Vast versus $1.19 on RunPod.
  - Source: [Neurohive, Oct 2026](https://neurohive.io/en/guides/cloud-gpu-pricing/)
  - Marketplaces are therefore roughly 2–6x cheaper than hyperscalers for an H100 hour.
- **Reliability:**
  - Vast.ai is a peer-to-peer model with "more variability in uptime and host responsiveness". This quote came from a search-result summary of 2026 comparison blogs; the exact page is unverified, possibly [Northflank](https://northflank.com/blog/cheapest-cloud-gpu-providers) or [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/cloud-gpu-pricing-comparison/).
  - Vast.ai's certification does not cover every machine. — [ComputePrices](https://computeprices.com/blog/gpu-marketplaces-compared-vast-runpod-salad-shadeform-and-more)
  - RunPod splits its supply into a Community Cloud (third-party hosts) and a Secure Cloud (its own datacenters). — [Spheron comparison](https://www.spheron.network/blog/runpod-vs-vastai-2026/). Vendor blog.
- **Interruption / spot:** spot capacity suits training with checkpoints every 15–30 minutes and inference burst capacity behind a load balancer. Multi-provider failover is becoming standard practice. — [Spheron, Apr/Oct 2026](https://www.spheron.network/blog/gpu-shortage-2026/). Vendor.
- **Interconnect:** large jobs need identical GPUs with high-speed interconnect, and some cheap per-GPU rates require renting a 4x or 8x node. — [ComputePrices](https://computeprices.com/blog/gpu-marketplaces-compared-vast-runpod-salad-shadeform-and-more)
- **Security / compliance:** enterprise buyers need "a known security posture". — [ComputePrices](https://computeprices.com/blog/gpu-marketplaces-compared-vast-runpod-salad-shadeform-and-more)
- **True cost:** spot availability and cross-AZ egress charges change the real cost of a GPU workload. — [Cast AI](https://cast.ai/blog/gpu-cloud-pricing/)

### Inferences
- The strongest fit for a spare-compute marketplace in 2026 is:
  - consumer and prosumer GPUs (4090/5090, 24–32GB) for open-weight LLM inference, LoRA fine-tuning, diffusion and video generation, and rendering;
  - batch and embarrassingly parallel jobs;
  - students and academics with 1–8 GPU needs.
- Enterprise production and multi-node training are a weak fit unless the marketplace offers verified "secure" tiers, SLAs, and clustered nodes.
- Buyers rank price highest, followed by immediate availability (no contracts or prepayment) and fast spin-up. The main reasons they churn are flaky hosts, slow data and checkpoint transfer, and privacy concerns.

### Gaps
- I found no independent survey quantifying the share of marketplace GPU-hours by segment (inference, fine-tuning, rendering, research, crypto/other).
- Render/VFX demand on marketplaces is not quantified here. Render Network's revenue claims are unverified.
- CPU-only rentable compute demand (scientific and batch CPU) was not covered by any source I found. Marketplace sources are almost entirely GPU-focused.

## 5. Developer sentiment about marketplace / community GPU clouds

### Takeaway
Developers on Hacker News (2026) see Vast.ai-style marketplaces as the default cheap option for experiments, personal LLM hosting, and one-off jobs. They praise very low prices and the variety of hardware. They complain about reliability, privacy ("random people's machines"), slow provisioning and data transfer, and wide price dispersion, and they generally will not run production on these platforms. Prices rose sharply in September–October 2026, and some developers noticed.

- A LessWrong shortform called Vast.ai "an unreliable mess with sometimes buggy not properly working servers and non-existent support", said the same of RunPod, and later noted that Vast had improved. — [LessWrong / GreaterWrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/4E2x8BZYJuzQGjmC6/luciaquirke-s-shortform)

### Inferences
- The opening is clear: buyers want marketplace prices with neocloud-like trust. In practice that means verified hosts, uptime scoring and refunds, encrypted or confidential execution, fast image and data loading (pre-cached models and checkpoints), and transparent, stable pricing.
- Host sentiment suggests supply is economically attractive when prices are high. A host earning $1–1.25/hr at about 100% utilization makes onboarding sellers easier in a tight market.

## 6. Shortage or glut in late 2026, and what it means for cheap marketplace compute

### Takeaway
As of late 2026 the market is in shortage, not glut. On-demand capacity was "sold out across all GPU types" by spring 2026. H100 one-year contract prices rose about 40% from Oct 2025 to Mar 2026. Neoclouds and hyperscalers raised list prices again in July and October 2026. A shortage with rising prices favours marketplaces that can bring idle and consumer GPUs online. The main risk is a later supply release as CoWoS and HBM capacity grows and Hopper inventory comes off contract, but that is not visible yet.

### Cited Findings
- **SemiAnalysis H100 one-year rental index:** **$1.70/GPU-hr (Oct 2025) to $2.35 (Mar 2026), about +40%**. "On-Demand GPU rental capacity is sold out across all GPU types."
  - Half of the providers surveyed could not supply 8 nodes (64 GPUs) of H100 or H200.
  - Blackwell lead times run into Jun–Jul 2026, and capacity coming online in Aug–Sep 2026 was already booked.
  - AWS p6-b200 spot instances were contested at about **$14/GPU-hr**, and some renters are subletting capacity.
  - H100s were renewing at their original rates, some through 2028.
  - Source: [SemiAnalysis, Apr 2 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-great-gpu-shortage-rental-capacity)
- **Price increases in H2 2026:**
  - On **Oct 1 2026**, Nebius raised H100 from $3.85 to **$4.50/hr**, H200 from $4.50 to $5.40, B200 from $7.15 to $8.50, and B300 from $7.85 to $9.50.
  - AWS Capacity Blocks rose about 15% in January, about 20% in July, and again in October.
  - RunPod and Lambda H100 SXM went from $2.99 or less in spring to **$3.99**, and RunPod B200 from about $5.89 to $7.99.
  - An RTX 5090 resold for about **$4,500 in Aug 2026**, more than twice its $1,999 MSRP.
  - Source: [Neurohive, Oct 2026](https://neurohive.io/en/guides/cloud-gpu-pricing/)
- CoreWeave raised list prices about 25% in July 2026 and called its capacity "effectively sold out". Nebius's October increase was about 17%. — [cloudmagazin, Oct 2026](https://www.cloudmagazin.com/en/2026/10/04/renting-gpus-without-hyperscalers-assessing-the-value-of-neo-clouds/)
- **Supply constraints** (Spheron, Apr 2026, updated Oct 2026; vendor source):
  - CoWoS capacity is estimated to rise from about 70–80K wafers/month (2025) to 120–140K by end-2026, narrowing the supply-demand gap from about 20% to about 10%.
  - HBM is concentrated in SK Hynix (about 50%) and Samsung (about 33%).
  - RTX 5000-series production was reportedly cut 30–40%.
  - ASE raised packaging quotes by more than 20% in July 2026.
  - H100 purchase lead times via resellers are quoted at 36–52 weeks, with other estimates ranging from 6 to 52 weeks.
  - Spot prices as of Sep 17 2026: H100 $2.60, B200 $5.22, RTX 4090 $0.48 on-demand.
  - Spheron expects top-end rates to stay flat through 2026, with Hopper softening before Blackwell.
  - Cash-settled H100/B200 compute futures were slated for Oct 2026.
  - Source: [Spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/gpu-shortage-2026/)
- **HN, Oct 17 2026:** Vast rates were "increasing like 50% in September alone". — [HN 50134971](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50134971)
- **Contrary view (outdated):** a January 2026 knowledge-base piece expected H100 prices to fall 10–20% in 2026 as Blackwell supply arrived. Events contradicted it. — [Cyfuture](https://cyfuture.cloud/kb/gpu/h100-gpu-price-trends-and-future-expectations)
- **Price indices diverge:** four GPU price indices disagree, and the market lacks a unified benchmark. — [KuCoin flash news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/gpu-pricing-disarray-four-indices-diverge-market-lacks-consensus). Low quality.

### Inferences
- A late-2026 shortage helps a spare-compute marketplace on both sides:
  - Buyers shut out of neocloud contracts (startups, academics, indie developers) need on-demand capacity without commitments.
  - Higher prices make it worthwhile for owners of idle 4090/5090/A100/H100 hardware to list it. The consumer-GPU squeeze (5090 resale above 2x MSRP) also pushes hobbyists toward renting.
- Timing risk: as CoWoS and HBM capacity grows through 2026–27 and Hopper fleets come off contract, rental prices could soften. That would narrow the marketplace price advantage, and the platform would then compete on reliability and UX rather than price alone.
- High prices make buyers more price-sensitive, so price transparency and stability could be a differentiator, given that "prices all over the place" is a common complaint.

### Gaps
- I found no public SemiAnalysis index value after March 2026. October 2026 levels are inferred from provider list-price changes.
- There is no hard utilization data for marketplace platforms (Vast, RunPod Community, Salad) in late 2026.
- I found no credible analyst forecast for when the shortage ends.
